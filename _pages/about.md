    <h1 id="intro-title"><strong>Soroush</strong> Esfahanian</h1>
    <p class="hero-affiliation">Research Intern, <a href="https://nlp.epfl.ch/">EPFL NLP Lab</a><br>Lausanne, Switzerland</p>
    <p class="intro">I want to understand how language models reason: the internal mechanisms behind their answers, how those mechanisms emerge through training, and why they sometimes fail.</p>
    <p>I am a final-year Computer Engineering student at the <a href="https://ut.ac.ir/en">University of Tehran</a> and a research intern at <a href="https://www.epfl.ch/">EPFL</a>, supervised by <a href="https://atcbosselut.github.io/">Prof. Antoine Bosselut</a>. My current work investigates how distillation configurations affect the reasoning capabilities transferred from teacher to student models.</p>
  </div>
  <figure class="portrait"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/prof_pic.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Soroush Esfahanian" width="640" height="800" fetchpriority="high"></figure>
  <div class="research-narrative">
    <p>I am particularly interested in connecting a model’s observable behavior to the computations that produce it. What representations and computational processes support multi-step reasoning? How do pretraining, post-training, and distillation shape them? When does a model’s written reasoning reflect the process that actually drives its answer? I want to investigate these questions through mechanistic interpretability and controlled experiments, using interventions to test explanations of model behavior. My longer-term goal is to turn that understanding into more reliable and generalizable reasoning.</p>
    <p>I am also curious about the relationship between model reasoning and human cognition, especially how models represent beliefs, intentions, and social situations. At <a href="https://teias.institute/">TeIAS</a>, I co-developed <a href="#publications">PerSo</a>, a benchmark for cognitive and behavioral social intelligence in LLMs. That work motivates my interest in bringing behavioral evaluation and mechanistic understanding together. Earlier, I worked on learning-based sound source localization at the University of Tehran’s <a href="https://taarlab.com/">Human and Robot Interaction Lab</a>.</p>
  </div>
